# Miracles you can witness

Gospel wonders in paint, and relics that still draw crowds

Note:
Christianity has told miracle stories for two thousand years, and some of them can still be visited: in museums, in churches, on fixed days of the year. This deck presents the tradition as believers hold it, and what sceptics and scientists say.

---

## Water into wine

- A wedding at Cana runs out of wine
- Six stone jars of water, and the best wine of the feast
- John calls it the first of Jesus' signs (John 2)
- Veronese painted it ten metres wide: the largest painting in the Louvre

![The Wedding at Cana, Paolo Veronese, 1563](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Les_Noces_de_Cana_-_Paolo_Veronese_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_INV_142_%3B_MR_384.jpg/1280px-Les_Noces_de_Cana_-_Paolo_Veronese_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_INV_142_%3B_MR_384.jpg)

Note:
Veronese turned a village wedding into a Venetian banquet with some 130 figures. It hangs opposite the Mona Lisa.

## Lazarus, come out

- Lazarus has been four days in the tomb at Bethany (John 11)
- “Jesus wept”: the shortest verse in the Bible
- Then he calls, and the dead man walks out in his grave clothes
- Giotto painted the onlookers covering their noses

![The Raising of Lazarus, Giotto, Scrovegni Chapel, Padua](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/55/Giotto_di_Bondone_-_No._25_Scenes_from_the_Life_of_Christ_-_9._Raising_of_Lazarus_-_WGA09204.jpg)

Note:
Giotto's fresco cycle in the Scrovegni Chapel in Padua (c. 1305) is one of the founding works of Western painting. Visits are timed and limited, which makes the chapel feel like a pilgrimage.

## Walking on water

- Night, a storm on the Sea of Galilee (Matthew 14)
- Jesus walks to the boat across the waves
- Peter steps out, walks, doubts and sinks
- Aivazovsky, a painter of the sea, gave it the light of a Russian icon

![Walking on Water, Ivan Aivazovsky, 1888](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/30/Po_vodam_1888.jpg)

Note:
The story is also in Mark 6 and John 6; only Matthew has Peter stepping out of the boat. It is read as a story about faith and fear: Peter walks while he looks at Jesus and sinks when he looks at the wind.

## Miracles that come back

### relics you can visit, on days you can put in a calendar

Note:
The Gospel miracles happened once. The next ones are claimed to repeat or to remain: blood that liquefies, flesh that endures, a cloth with an image. The Church treats these as signs that may help faith, not articles of faith: Catholics are not required to believe in any of them.

---

## The blood of San Gennaro

- Januarius, bishop of Benevento, martyred around 305
- Two glass ampoules of his dried blood, in Naples Cathedral
- It is said to liquefy three times a year: May, 19 September, 16 December
- First recorded in 1389; when it stays solid, Naples fears the worst

![The reliquary that carries the ampoules in procession](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Reliquiario_di_Roberto_d%27Angio.jpg/1280px-Reliquiario_di_Roberto_d%27Angio.jpg)

Note:
The May date is the Saturday before the first Sunday of May; 19 September is his feast day; 16 December remembers the eruption of Vesuvius in 1631. The archbishop holds up the reliquary and announces when the blood has turned liquid; the crowd cheers. A failure has been linked in popular memory to disasters, such as the 1980 Irpinia earthquake.

---

## What science says

- The ampoules have never been opened, so the contents are untested
- 1991, in _Nature_: three Italian chemists made a gel from iron salts, chalk and salt
- It is thixotropic: solid at rest, liquid when moved, like the relic
- The Church has never formally declared it a miracle, and lets the devotion continue

Note:
Luigi Garlaschelli, Franco Ramaccini and Sergio Della Sala, "Working bloody miracles", Nature, 1991. Iron chloride occurs naturally near Vesuvius, so a medieval recipe is plausible. Believers answer that a hypothesis is not a proof, and that the timing and the crowds are the point.

## Lanciano, 8th century

- A monk doubts that the bread is Christ's body
- At his Mass the host becomes flesh and the wine becomes blood
- Both are still on display in the church of San Francesco
- A 1970s study by Odoardo Linoli: human heart muscle, blood type AB

![The altar of the Eucharistic miracle, San Francesco, Lanciano](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Lanciano_-_Chiesa_di_San_Francesco%2C_altare_del_miracolo_eucaristico_01.JPG/1280px-Lanciano_-_Chiesa_di_San_Francesco%2C_altare_del_miracolo_eucaristico_01.JPG)

Note:
The blood is said to have clotted into five pellets. Linoli's analysis (1970–71) is cited by believers; sceptics point out that it was never independently repeated or published in a major scientific journal, and that the early history of the relic is known only from later sources.

## Bolsena, 1263

- A priest from Bohemia doubts, on his way to Rome
- At Mass in Bolsena, blood drips from the host onto the altar cloth
- The cloth is taken to Pope Urban IV, at nearby Orvieto
- In 1264 he makes Corpus Christi a feast of the whole Church

![The Mass at Bolsena, Raphael, 1512](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Raphael_-_The_Mass_at_Bolsena.jpg/1280px-Raphael_-_The_Mass_at_Bolsena.jpg)

Note:
Raphael painted the scene for the Vatican, with Pope Julius II kneeling in it. Historians note that the feast had been promoted for decades by Juliana of Liège, and that the link between the miracle and the feast is first told in later sources.

![Orvieto Cathedral, built to house the cloth of Bolsena](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Facciata_Duomo_di_Orvieto.jpg/1280px-Facciata_Duomo_di_Orvieto.jpg)

Note:
Orvieto Cathedral was begun in 1290; the stained cloth (the corporal) is kept in its own chapel and carried through the town on Corpus Christi. The feast is still celebrated with street processions, flower carpets and folk costume, as here in Łowicz.

## The Shroud of Turin

- 4.4 metres of linen with the faint image of a crucified man
- 1898: Secondo Pia's photographic negative shows a lifelike face
- 1988: carbon dating in three labs puts the linen at 1260–1390
- Defenders dispute the samples; the Church calls it an icon and makes no ruling

![Secondo Pia's 1898 negative of the face on the Shroud](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Secundo_Pia_Turinske_platno_1898.jpg/1280px-Secundo_Pia_Turinske_platno_1898.jpg)

Note:
The image behaves like a negative, which nobody expected before Pia's photograph. The radiocarbon tests (Oxford, Arizona and Zurich) were published in Nature in 1989. The cloth is first documented in Lirey, France, in the 1350s; it has been in Turin since 1578 and is shown to the public only in rare expositions.
