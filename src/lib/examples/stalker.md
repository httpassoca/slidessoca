# Stalker

Andrei Tarkovsky, 1979

Note:
Stalker (Сталкер) was made at Mosfilm and released in 1979: 161 minutes, long takes, almost no plot. This deck walks through what happens, what it might mean, and how it was made.

---

## A film about a room

- Three men walk into a forbidden place
- They want to reach a room that grants wishes
- Nobody goes in

![Andrei Tarkovsky, portrait by Boris Baldin](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b5/Andrei_Tarkovsky_by_Boris_Baldin_%28cropped%29.jpg)

Note:
Tarkovsky (1932–1986) made seven features. Stalker is the last one he made in the Soviet Union; Nostalghia and The Sacrifice were made abroad.

---

## Where it comes from

- _Roadside Picnic_ (1972), a novel by Arkady and Boris Strugatsky
- Aliens visited Earth, left, and dropped their litter
- The Zones they left are deadly; stalkers smuggle out what's inside
- The brothers wrote the script, draft after draft, until the science fiction was almost gone

Note:
The novel's title is the joke: humans are the ants crawling over what the visitors left after a picnic. The film keeps the Zone, the stalker and the wish-granting object (a golden sphere in the book) and drops almost everything else.

## The Zone was a real place

### the abandoned hydro plant on the Jägala river

![The abandoned hydroelectric plant on the Jägala river, Estonia](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/J%C3%A4gala_HEP%2C_Sept_2009.jpg/1280px-J%C3%A4gala_HEP%2C_Sept_2009.jpg)

Note:
Inside the film, the Zone is a cordoned-off area where something happened. Nobody knows what. The traps move; the straight path is the deadly one. The Stalker tests the way by throwing metal nuts tied to strips of cloth. Outside the film, it is the Jägala river, about 25 km east of Tallinn, with its deserted hydroelectric plants.

---

## Three men

- **The Stalker**, the guide. Poor, devout, a little broken (Alexander Kaidanovsky)
- **The Writer**, famous, bored, out of ideas (Anatoly Solonitsyn)
- **The Professor**, a physicist with a secret in his rucksack (Nikolai Grinko)

Note:
They have no names. The Writer wants his inspiration back, or says he does. The Professor says he wants a discovery. Only the Stalker has nothing to gain: he cannot use the Room for himself.

---

## The Room

- It grants your innermost wish, not the one you say out loud
- Porcupine, the Stalker's teacher, went in to bring his brother back
- He came home rich instead, and hanged himself
- The Room knows you better than you know yourself

Note:
This is the real threat of the Room, and why both visitors stop at the door. If it gives you what you truly want, going in means finding out who you are.

---

> Weakness is a great thing, and strength is nothing.
> — The Stalker, after the Tao Te Ching

Note:
From the Stalker's prayer early in the journey, adapted from chapter 76 of the Tao Te Ching: when a man is born he is soft and supple, when he dies he is hard and stiff. Hardness belongs to death, softness to life. Translations of the line vary.

---

## Faith against reason

- The Professor brings a bomb to destroy the Room, then takes it apart
- The Writer mocks the pilgrimage, then refuses to enter
- The film never shows whether the Room works
- What breaks the Stalker is not danger but that nobody believes

Note:
Tarkovsky called it a film about faith. You can read it as a believer against two sceptics, or as three men who end up at the same threshold, afraid of the same thing.

---

## Shot twice in Estonia

- Planned for Isfara, Tajikistan, until a 1976 earthquake moved it to Estonia
- In 1977 most of a year's footage was ruined in processing
- Tarkovsky reshot it in 1978 with a new cinematographer
- A Tallinn power plant became the edge of the Zone. Its chimney still says UN

![The UN letters painted on the chimney of the Tallinn power plant](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/UN_Sign_on_the_chimney_from_the_movie_Stalker_Tallinn_1_October_2016.jpg/1280px-UN_Sign_on_the_chimney_from_the_movie_Stalker_Tallinn_1_October_2016.jpg)

Note:
The first cinematographer, Georgy Rerberg, left after the lab disaster (the new Kodak stock was developed wrongly). Alexander Knyazhinsky shot the version we know. The Tallinn power plant is now a museum and arts centre; the UN letters painted for the film are still on the chimney.

---

## A toxic location

- The Zone scenes were filmed by the Jägala river, below a chemical plant
- The crew remembered foam and fumes on the water
- Tarkovsky (1986), Solonitsyn (1982) and Larisa Tarkovskaya (1998) all died of cancer
- Sound designer Vladimir Sharun blamed the location. It was never proven

![An abandoned building in the old Tallinn gas plant area](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Abandoned_building_Tallinn_gas_plant_area.jpg/1280px-Abandoned_building_Tallinn_gas_plant_area.jpg)

Note:
Sharun's recollection: upriver a chemical plant poured poisonous liquids downstream, there was white foam on the water, and "many people in our crew later died of cancer". It is one witness's belief, not a documented finding. The photo shows a similar abandoned site in Tallinn, not the set itself.

---

## The ending

- The Stalker's daughter, Monkey, cannot walk: a child of the Zone
- Alone at the table, she recites a poem by Tyutchev
- She looks at three glasses and they slide across the table
- A train passes, Beethoven's Ode to Joy under the noise

Note:
A miracle, or the vibration of the train? Everyone who went to the Room came back with nothing, and the one miracle happens at home, to someone who never went. The film lets you choose, which is the point.
