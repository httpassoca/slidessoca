# Why democracy fails

2,400 years of warnings, and why it keeps surviving

Note:
A tour of the classic arguments for why popular government breaks down, from Plato to political science, and the evidence on the other side. No current politicians, no parties: the ideas only.

---

## The Pnyx, Athens

### where citizens voted, and where the worry began

![The Pnyx plateau with the Acropolis behind it](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/The_Pnyx_plateau_and_the_Acropolis_on_August_21%2C_2019.jpg/1280px-The_Pnyx_plateau_and_the_Acropolis_on_August_21%2C_2019.jpg)

Note:
The Athenian assembly met on this hill. Every citizen (adult free men, a minority of the population) could speak and vote. The same assembly sentenced Socrates to death in 399 BC, which is where Plato's distrust of democracy starts.

---

## Plato: too much freedom

- _Republic_, Book VIII: constitutions decay in a fixed order
- Timocracy → oligarchy → democracy → tyranny
- Freedom without limits breeds a crowd-pleaser
- The crowd-pleaser becomes the tyrant

![Bust of Plato, Roman copy after Silanion](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/Plato_Silanion_Musei_Capitolini_MC1377.jpg/1280px-Plato_Silanion_Musei_Capitolini_MC1377.jpg)

Note:
Plato's line is that tyranny arises from no other constitution than democracy: an excess of liberty turns into an excess of slavery. He was writing a philosophical argument, not a history, but the pattern he describes (a popular champion who asks for a bodyguard) became a template.

## Polybius: the cycle

- _Histories_, Book VI: anacyclosis
- Monarchy → tyranny → aristocracy → oligarchy → democracy → mob rule → monarchy
- Every pure form rots into its bad twin
- His cure: Rome's mixed constitution, consuls, Senate and people checking each other

![Relief of Polybius from Kleitor](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/66/Relief_aus_Kleitor.JPG)

Note:
Polybius was a Greek hostage in Rome in the 2nd century BC. He thought the cycle was close to a law of nature, and that only a balance of powers could slow it down. The idea passed through Machiavelli and Montesquieu to the authors of the US Constitution.

## Madison: faction

- _Federalist_ No. 10, 1787
- A faction: citizens united by a passion or interest against the rights of others
- You cannot remove the causes without removing liberty
- So control the effects: representation, and a republic large enough that no faction dominates

![James Madison, by Gilbert Stuart](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Gilbert_Stuart%2C_James_Madison%2C_c._1821%2C_NGA_56914.jpg/1280px-Gilbert_Stuart%2C_James_Madison%2C_c._1821%2C_NGA_56914.jpg)

Note:
Madison's argument turned the conventional wisdom upside down: people believed republics only worked when small. He argued a large one was safer, because more interests make a stable majority of oppressors harder to form.

---

> The latent causes of faction are thus sown in the nature of man.
> — James Madison, Federalist No. 10

Note:
The essay was published in November 1787 under the pen name Publius, as part of the campaign to ratify the US Constitution.

## Tocqueville: the tyranny of the majority

- _Democracy in America_, 1835 and 1840
- The danger is not a king, it is the majority's hold on opinion
- And a soft despotism: citizens trading liberty for comfort, letting a mild state think for them

![Alexis de Tocqueville, by Théodore Chassériau](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/aa/Alexis_de_tocqueville.jpg)

Note:
Tocqueville admired American democracy and studied it closely. His warning is about what equal citizens might choose freely: conformity and dependence, not chains.

## Rational ignorance

- Anthony Downs, _An Economic Theory of Democracy_, 1957
- One vote almost never decides an election
- So studying the issues costs more than it pays back
- Voters stay uninformed on purpose, which leaves room for demagogues and organised interests

Note:
Downs did not say voters are stupid. He said ignorance is a rational response to the incentives. It is one of the central problems of public choice theory, and a reason institutions matter more than any individual voter's knowledge.

## Weimar, 1933

- 30 January: Hitler is appointed chancellor by constitutional means
- 27 February: the Reichstag burns; the next day a decree suspends civil rights
- 23 March: the Enabling Act passes 444 to 94
- Parliament voted its own power away. No coup was needed

![The Reichstag on fire, 27 February 1933](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Reichstagsbrand.jpg/1280px-Reichstagsbrand.jpg)

Note:
The Enabling Act let the government make laws without the Reichstag. Communist deputies had already been arrested or driven out; the Social Democrats were the only party to vote against. The lesson most often drawn: democracies can be ended through their own procedures.

## How democracies die

- Steven Levitsky and Daniel Ziblatt, 2018
- Most modern breakdowns start at the ballot box, not with tanks
- The guardrails are unwritten norms
- **Mutual toleration**: rivals are legitimate, not enemies
- **Forbearance**: not using every legal power to its limit

Note:
Their book compares cases across Europe and Latin America. Parties also act as gatekeepers: when they refuse to ally with extremists, the guardrails hold; when they think they can control them, the guardrails fail.

---

## And yet, they survive

- Elections remove leaders without violence, again and again
- A free press and courts expose mistakes early
- Amartya Sen: no major famine in a democracy with a free press
- Przeworski and colleagues: rich democracies have almost never collapsed
- 1989 showed that the cycle can also turn the other way

![Crowds at the Brandenburg Gate and the Berlin Wall, December 1989](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/BrandenburgerTorDezember1989.jpg/1280px-BrandenburgerTorDezember1989.jpg)

Note:
The counter-case is that democracy's weaknesses come with a correction mechanism: bad decisions are visible, and governments can be replaced. The warnings above are about what happens when that mechanism itself is taken apart.
